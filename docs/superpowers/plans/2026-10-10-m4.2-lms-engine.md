# M4.2 LMS Engine and Percentile Gate Implementation Plan

**Goal:** Work out a girl's z-score and percentile for weight, length and head circumference from WHO's LMS tables. Then prove it on every day from 0 to 730 against WHO's own published percentiles and SD values, so that M4.4 may show percentiles.

**Architecture:**
- **Age:** `ageInDays` in `packages/domain/src/growth/age.ts` counts whole calendar days between two local dates.
- **The LMS method:**
  - `zScoreLMS` in `growth/z-score.ts` applies WHO's formula, with its restricted application beyond ±3 SD.
  - `percentile` in `growth/normal-cdf.ts` is the normal distribution, by Marsaglia's series.
- **The entry points:** `zScoreFor` and `percentileFor` in `growth/percentile-for.ts`.
  - They take a measurement as growth records store it, and look up the day's L, M and S with M4.1's `girlsLms`.
  - They answer a number, or `"unavailable"`.
- **The gate:** `test/growth/who-gate.test.ts` reads WHO's six bundled tables back through `percentileFor` and `zScoreFor` for every day. `docs/percentiles.md` records the method and the tolerances.

**Tech Stack:** TypeScript 5.9.3, Vitest 4.1.11 with fast-check, oxlint 1.87.0 and oxfmt 0.72.0, Node 24.21.0. No new dependency.

## Decisions this plan makes (veto in review)

- **P1: Age is chronological, in whole calendar days** (Andrew, 10 Oct 2026).
  - `ageInDays(measuredOn, birthDate)` takes two local dates and subtracts their UTC midnights, so no host zone or clock change can leave a fraction.
  - It throws `RangeError` on a string that isn't a real date, as `addDaysToDate` does.
  - It goes negative before birth, where `girlsLms` has nothing.
  - Gestational age plays no part. WHO's standards describe babies born at term, and this child was born at 281 days (§3.3), 40 weeks and 1 day.
- **P2: `zScoreLMS(value, lms)` takes M4.1's `Lms`,** not L, M and S apart as §3.1 wrote them, so the three can't be swapped.
  - The formula is z = ((x / M)^L − 1) / (L × S), or ln(x / M) / S when L is 0.
  - WHO's girls' tables never hold L = 0: weight's L runs from 0.3809 to −0.294 without landing on it. A boys' table could.
  - It answers `NaN` for a value that isn't above 0.
- **P3: WHO's restricted application, for every metric.**
  - Beyond ±3, z = ±3 + (x − SD±3) / (the distance from SD±2 to SD±3), where SDk = M(1 + L × S × k)^(1/L).
  - With L = 1, as for length and head circumference, it equals the curve's own z. So no metric needs a branch.
  - WHO's SD4 and SD4neg columns hold the restricted values. Take weight on day 0:
    - WHO's SD4 is 5.356.
    - The restricted value is 5.3559.
    - The curve's own would be 5.4000.
- **P4: `percentile(z)` sums Marsaglia's series** (Journal of Statistical Software 11(4), 2004).
  - The paper's C function is good to an absolute 8 × 10⁻¹⁶.
  - This port works in JavaScript's doubles. Task 3 holds it to 12 decimals of a percentile, against values from `erfc`.
  - It answers 0 from −8 down and 100 from 8 up, where the tail is under 1e-13 points. It answers `NaN` for `NaN`. The cutoffs also keep the loop short and its terms finite.
  - An erfc approximation such as Abramowitz and Stegun 7.1.26 would need no loop. But it is good only to 1.5 × 10⁻⁷, and it needs more constants than Marsaglia's one.
- **P5: `zScoreFor(metric, sex, ageDays, value)` and `percentileFor` take the value as growth records store it.**
  - That's grams for weight, and centimetres for length and head circumference: M4.3's `weight_g`, `length_cm` and `head_cm`.
  - A weight is divided by 1,000 into WHO's kilograms.
  - They answer `"unavailable"` in three cases:
    - for `"male"`
    - for an age `girlsLms` has no row for
    - for a value that isn't a positive finite number
  - Results are unrounded. M4.4 rounds a percentile to a whole number for display.
  - `sex` is typed `"female" | "male"` in place, so the domain needn't export a second `Sex` beside `@bt/contracts`' one.
- **P6: The gate is `packages/domain/test/growth/who-gate.test.ts`,** with 42 tests in each host zone:
  - 15 that read WHO's P3, P15, P50, P85 and P97 for each metric through `percentileFor`, within ±0.5 points, as the M4.2 row asks
  - 27 that read WHO's SD4neg to SD4 through `zScoreFor`, within ±0.002 in z
  - Each test lists the days that miss, so a failure names them.
  - It is the whole gate. CI runs it on every PR, and nothing in the app switches percentiles off (Andrew, 10 Oct 2026).
- **P7: Why ±0.002 in z, not ±0.01.**
  - WHO prints each value to 3 decimals. That moves z by up to 0.0014 (weight SD3neg, day 7).
  - Reading the next day's L, M and S misses by at least 0.0022, on every day and in every SD column but SD0 (head circumference SD4, day 723).
  - M4.1's final review suggested "within a hundredth". That would let a late one-day shift through.
- **P8: Days after 730 stay `"unavailable"`** (Andrew, 10 Oct 2026: revisit before the second birthday).
  - The gate runs to `WHO_LAST_DAY`, so extending the tables needs no change to it.
- **P9: Sizes.**
  - No app imports the new functions yet, so both bundles are unchanged.
  - M4.4 works out percentiles on the phone (Andrew, 10 Oct 2026). So `apps/web` will import `@bt/domain` directly and gain `lms-girls.json`'s 12.9 KiB gzip (M4.1's measure). The Worker gains nothing.
- **P10: Docs.**
  - `docs/percentiles.md` holds the method, the tolerances, the worst errors seen and the sources.
  - §3.1, the M4.2 row and §8's risk row point at it.

## Review Focus

1. **An age counted in hours, not calendar days.** A measurement two days after the birth date, across a daylight-saving change, would come out as 1.96 or 2.04 days, and every percentile would be "unavailable". Expect whole days in any host zone. Tested in Task 1 ("counts two whole days across …"), which runs under Australia/Lord_Howe and America/Adak.
2. **A table read a day out of step.** Expect each day's own L, M and S. Tested in Task 4 ("uses the L, M and S of the day it's given") and Task 5, whose ±0.002 z-score check a one-day shift fails (P7).
3. **A weight in grams handed to WHO's kilograms.** Expect grams in, and kilograms to the LMS. Tested in Task 4 ("reads a weight in grams, …") and Task 5 (every weight column).
4. **A measurement far beyond 3 SD,** such as a weight typed as 35 kg. Expect WHO's restricted z-score and a percentile from 0 to 100, never `NaN`. Tested in Task 2 (the restricted steps), Task 3 (0 and 100 beyond ±8, ±Infinity included) and Task 5 (SD4neg and SD4).
5. **A measurement no percentile describes.** That means a boy, an age before birth, after day 730 or part of a day, or a value of zero, below zero, `NaN` or infinity. Expect `"unavailable"`, never a number. Tested in Task 2 (`NaN` for zero, below zero and `NaN`) and Task 4 ("is unavailable for …").

```ts
import type { Lms } from "./who-lms.ts"

const LAST_SD_ON_THE_CURVE = 3

const boxCoxZ = ({ L, M, S }: Lms, value: number): number =>
  L === 0 ? Math.log(value / M) / S : ((value / M) ** L - 1) / (L * S)

const valueAtZ = ({ L, M, S }: Lms, z: number): number =>
  L === 0 ? M * Math.exp(S * z) : M * (1 + L * S * z) ** (1 / L)

export const zScoreLMS = (value: number, lms: Lms): number => {
  if (!(value > 0)) return Number.NaN
  const z = boxCoxZ(lms, value)
  if (Math.abs(z) <= LAST_SD_ON_THE_CURVE) return z
  const side = Math.sign(z)
  const outer = valueAtZ(lms, side * LAST_SD_ON_THE_CURVE)
  const inner = valueAtZ(lms, side * (LAST_SD_ON_THE_CURVE - 1))
  return side * LAST_SD_ON_THE_CURVE + (value - outer) / (side * (outer - inner))
}
```

```ts
const LN_SQRT_TWO_PI = 0.9189385332046728
const NEGLIGIBLE_TAIL_Z = 8

const marsagliaSeries = (z: number): number => {
  const square = z * z
  let term = z
  let sum = z
  for (let k = 3; Math.abs(term) > Number.EPSILON * Math.abs(sum); k += 2) {
    term *= square / k
    sum += term
  }
  return sum
}

export const percentile = (z: number): number => {
  if (Number.isNaN(z)) return Number.NaN
  if (z <= -NEGLIGIBLE_TAIL_Z) return 0
  if (z >= NEGLIGIBLE_TAIL_Z) return 100
  return 100 * (0.5 + marsagliaSeries(z) * Math.exp(-0.5 * z * z - LN_SQRT_TWO_PI))
}
```

```ts
import { percentile } from "./normal-cdf.ts"
import { girlsLms, type GrowthMetric } from "./who-lms.ts"
import { zScoreLMS } from "./z-score.ts"

type Sex = "female" | "male"

const RECORDED_PER_WHO_UNIT = {
  weightForAge: 1000,
  lengthForAge: 1,
  headCircumferenceForAge: 1,
} satisfies { readonly [metric in GrowthMetric]: number }

export const zScoreFor = (metric: GrowthMetric, sex: Sex, ageDays: number, value: number): number | "unavailable" => {
  const lms = sex === "female" ? girlsLms(metric, ageDays) : null
  if (lms === null || !(Number.isFinite(value) && value > 0)) return "unavailable"
  return zScoreLMS(value / RECORDED_PER_WHO_UNIT[metric], lms)
}

export const percentileFor = (
  metric: GrowthMetric,
  sex: Sex,
  ageDays: number,
  value: number,
): number | "unavailable" => {
  const z = zScoreFor(metric, sex, ageDays, value)
  return z === "unavailable" ? z : percentile(z)
}
```

```markdown
# Percentiles

The app works out a growth percentile only from WHO's Child Growth Standards for girls, from birth to day 730. `packages/domain/test/growth/who-gate.test.ts` reads WHO's own tables back through the engine for every one of those days. Percentiles stay off the screen while it fails (M4.2).

## How a percentile is worked out

1. **Age:** `ageInDays(measuredOn, birthDate)` counts whole calendar days from the birth date to the measurement's local date in the household's time zone. It is chronological age, so gestational age at birth plays no part (decided 10 Oct 2026). WHO's standards describe babies born at term, and the child was born at 281 days, 40 weeks and 1 day.
2. **L, M and S:** `girlsLms(metric, ageDays)` reads WHO's values for that day from `data/who/lms-girls.json` (M4.1).
3. **z-score:** `zScoreLMS(value, lms)` applies WHO's LMS formula, z = ((x / M)^L − 1) / (L × S), or ln(x / M) / S when L is 0. Beyond ±3 it follows WHO's restricted application: past 3 SD, each further SD is as wide as the step from 2 SD to 3 SD. Only weight's L differs from 1, so only weight's z-scores change.
4. **Percentile:** `percentile(z)` is 100 × Φ(z), the standard normal distribution, from Marsaglia's series. Beyond ±8 it answers 0 or 100, which is within 1e-13 points.

`zScoreFor(metric, sex, ageDays, value)` and `percentileFor` run these steps. They take a measurement in the unit growth records store: grams for weight, and centimetres for length and head circumference. A weight is divided by 1,000 into WHO's kilograms.

## When there is no percentile

`percentileFor` answers `"unavailable"`, which the app shows as "Percentile unavailable", for:

- a boy, since only girls' tables are bundled
- an age that isn't a whole day from 0 to 730, such as a two-year visit on day 731 after a 29 February
- a measurement that isn't a positive number

## The gate

| Check, on every day from 0 to 730 | Tolerance | Worst seen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WHO's P3, P15, P50, P85 and P97, through `percentileFor` | ±0.5 percentile points | 0.042: weight, P50, day 3 |
| WHO's SD4neg to SD4, through `zScoreFor` | ±0.002 in z | 0.0014: weight, SD3neg, day 7 |

- **SD4 and SD4neg test the restricted application.** WHO's values for them are its restricted ones. Without the restriction, weight's SD4neg misses by up to 0.17 (day 728).
```
